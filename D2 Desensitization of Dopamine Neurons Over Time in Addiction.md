# Overview/Description
D2 neuron desensitization by downregulation of D2 neuron receptors due to repeated large dopamine bursts created during addiction and the use of addictive substances. 
The D2 receptor network's functionality is to dampen and inhibit activity whenever dopamine influxes are present in the brain due to changes in the external environment's activities. 
D2 neurons act as modulators for dopamine when it is released, and in addition, they regulate goal-related activities, decision-making, and self-control.
Over time, the removal of receptors leads to the brain's inability to properly regulate dopamine, promoting impulsivity in human behavior and impairing the ability to suppress actions and exercise self-control.
